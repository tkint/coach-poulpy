# Poulpy in 3D

The plush as a solid you can turn around: a bubble of a head tucked into eight puffy arms, drawn with flat cartoon shading. One file, [`design/poulpy-3d/model.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/model.mjs), describes his shape; the [Poulpy in 3D](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-in-3d--docs) page of the component workshop draws it live, with a page for each of [his moves](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-in-3d--docs), and [`design/build-poulpy-3d.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/build-poulpy-3d.mjs) writes it as glTF files. He is not in the apps yet: this is the reference to build from.

## The rules of the shape

These were settled with the owner on the first model; the reasons are in [Brand decisions](?path=/docs/brand-decisions--docs#14-poulpy-in-3d-a-bubble-plush-in-flat-shading).

- **Chibi, never realistic.** A big round head, big eyes, short arms. The face is the 2D drawing's, pressed onto the head, so every [drawing](?path=/docs/brand-drawings--docs) and the model show the same Poulpy.
- **The head is tucked in.** It sinks into the arms, which wrap around its base, instead of resting on top of them.
- **Eight arms, always down.** They lie on the floor as on the plush; a mood shows on his face and in his colour, never by lifting an arm. When he [jumps](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-little-jumps--docs) they hang under him.
- **The other side is underneath.** Each arm is one smooth shape, the side's colour on top and the other side's colour below a low line, with no ridge or shelf between them. The base of the head is cut at the same height, so the whole underside is one colour: ember under the blue side, blue under the ember side.
- **Flat, like a drawing.** Plain token colours, one soft shadow band and one faint gleam. No gloss, no reflections, no fabric texture.
- **The light belongs to the room.** It comes from the viewer's upper left and stays there: the shade and the gleam slide over Poulpy as he turns, they are never painted on him.
- **The outline is optional and off by default.** It is there for small sizes or busy backgrounds.


## His six moods

The model makes the six [moods](?path=/docs/brand-moods--docs) of the drawings: the same faces, pressed onto the head feature by feature, and the same shades, from the `mood` tokens. The side of the plush follows the mood, so the three ember moods show blue underneath.

<Story of={Live.Moods} />

- **The faces are the drawings'.** Each face in `model.mjs` is written in the 2D drawing's own coordinates, so the two can be read side by side and a change to one is copied to the other.
- **A mood changes his face and his shade, never his shape.** Heavy lids, brows, a smirk or clenched teeth lie on the head like the eyes do; his arms stay down.


## His twelve poses

The model also takes the twelve [poses](?path=/docs/brand-moods--docs) of the drawings, each in the shade of its mood. They are in [`design/poulpy-3d/poses.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/poses.mjs), built on the shape and the faces of `model.mjs`.

<Story of={Live.Poses} />

- **What he wears is solid.** The sweatband goes all the way around his head, the nightcap sits on it, the medal rests on his front arm and the dumbbell lies on the floor beside him: turn him and they turn with him.
- **What is on him is on his skin.** The plasters and the vein are laid against his head where the drawing has them and wrapped around it; sweat and tears are part of his face. Turn him and they stay where they are.
- **The comic effects stay flat and face the viewer.** Sparks, confetti, puffs, bubbles and lettering are the drawing's own, standing in front of him or behind him, and they keep facing whoever looks while he turns.
- **Cocky has no halo and no "PR!" badge in 3D.** The owner dropped them; his sparks stay, and become solid stars when he [winks](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-the-cocky-wink--docs).
- **He still never lifts an arm.** Standing tall stretches his arms taller and lifts his head with them; Side glance leans his head. Nothing else moves.
- **The loader is his outline from underneath, drawn frame by frame, with loose tentacles.** It is made from the model itself, seen from straight below, with his tentacles wider and shorter than in the other pictures so that neighbours touch at their base. Only the edge of his shape is drawn, in the brand blue and see-through, as the old loader was, rounded, with no line inside. Each tentacle is loose: it bends on the same arm springs as his turn (`createFollow` in `motion.mjs`) and trails behind the turn. A cycle is 48 frames in one sheet, showing how the tentacles hang at that moment relative to his body; the apps and the website play them in turn and turn the whole thing smoothly. They are kept once, in `design/poulpy-3d/loader`, and every platform reads them from there. He swings half a turn one way and then half a turn the other (from -180° to 180°), forever, once every 3.6 s (once every 12 s with reduced motion), easing at each end. The other version only turns clockwise, a full turn every 3.6 s, with his tentacles trailing the same all the time, so it is one picture (`clockwise` on the phones, `spin="clockwise"` on the web). Which frame and how far it is turned is `LoaderSwing` in the shared Kotlin, and `PoulpyLoader` on Android and iOS, `MobilePoulpyLoader` and `CpPoulpyLoader` in the workshop all follow it.


## His pictures

Where the apps and the website do not need him live, they take a picture of him: one for each of the six moods and twelve poses, painted from the model, and the frames of the loader. They are in [`design/poulpy-3d/pictures`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/pictures), one PNG each at 744 by 678 pixels, the same frame as the drawings they replace so a picture can take a drawing's place without moving anything. **They are transparent around him**, with no background and no tile, so they sit on a light or a dark screen alike; the preview below shows them on the page's own background.

<Story of={Live.Pictures} />

- **They are painted by our own script.** [`design/poulpy-3d/picture.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/picture.mjs) draws what `model.mjs` and `poses.mjs` build, pixel by pixel, in plain JavaScript with the look of `LOOK`: the same flat shading, gleam and floor shadow as the live view. No browser or graphics card is involved, so every machine writes the same pixels, as for the app icons.
- **Every picture is taken from the same place.** He is seen from a little above and face on, close enough to fill the frame, and the same distance for all of them so he is the same size from one to the next.
- **The floor shadow is part of the picture**: a see-through dark blue that fades to nothing, so it shows on a light screen and disappears on a dark one.
- **Generated, never edited.** `node design/build-poulpy-pictures.mjs` writes them and CI runs it with `--check`. Change the model, a face or a pose, then regenerate.


## His moves

He moves in 3D too. Each move has its own page in the component workshop, so only one runs at a time:

- [Turning in short steps](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-turning--docs): his idle.
- [Little jumps](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-little-jumps--docs) on the spot.
- [Jumping around](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-jumping-around--docs): the same jumps, each turning him.
- [The champion jumps for joy](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-the-champion--docs), his medal bouncing and confetti flying.
- [The cocky one winks](?path=/docs/brand-poulpy-moves-the-cocky-wink--docs), and stars pop up around him.

All of them are one file, [`design/poulpy-3d/motion.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/motion.mjs), next to the shape. A preview only moves while it is on screen, and starts paused for anyone who asked their device for less motion; an app must do the same.

| [`design/poulpy-3d/poses.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/poses.mjs) | His twelve poses, the same way |
| [`design/poulpy-3d/motion.mjs`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/poulpy-3d/motion.mjs) | How he moves, the same way |

The glTF files open in any 3D tool or engine. Poulpy stands on the floor (y = 0, y up) and faces +z; his head is about one unit from centre to edge. In the two files that stand still, each part is its own named mesh (`head`, `head_lining`, `arm_1` to `arm_8` and their `_lining`, `eye_left`, `eye_left_shine`, `cheek_left`, `mouth`, `gleam`, plus `brow_left` and `brow_right` on the ember side; left and right are Poulpy's own), so a part can be recoloured, hidden or animated on its own.

The two `_turn` files hold the same meshes, rigged. The node `turn` faces him and `squash` under it carries his head and face; each arm is skinned to `turn` and to its own chain of three joints, `arm_1_bend_1` to `arm_1_bend_3` from the head outwards. The animation `turn` plays one full turn at 30 frames a second and ends where it starts, so any player can loop it. It is a recording of the springs at their design values: an engine that wants them live, to change the softness or to react to something, runs `createTurn()` from `motion.mjs` on the same joints, as the workshop's preview does.

The `jump` and `cheer` files use the same rig and move more of it: `turn` also lifts and tilts him, `squash` also dips his head into his arms, and the first joint of each arm slides outwards as he tucks and bends down as it hangs. In `cheer` the medal hangs from three more nodes, `medal_sway` (it slides around him), `medal_hinge` (it swings up off his arm) and `medal_rest` (it holds its parts). Both play three rounds of jumps in a loop at 30 frames a second, recorded after the springs have settled. The confetti and the comic effects are not in the files: they are flat cards that keep facing the viewer, so they belong to whoever draws him, as the look does. The cocky wink is not in a file either: it changes the shape of his face as it goes, which glTF would only carry as one mesh per step.

A glTF file only carries shapes and plain matte colours. The look is the renderer's job, and `LOOK` in `model.mjs` gives its numbers: the light's direction as the viewer sees it, the shadow band's strength, where the gleam sits, and the outline's width and tint. A renderer that wants the gleam to follow the light drops the `gleam` mesh from the file and asks `gleamPatch(direction)` for a new one whenever Poulpy or the camera turns. [`poulpy3dScene.ts`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/web/storybook/stories/brand/poulpy3dScene.ts), which draws the workshop's preview with three.js, is the worked example.


## Changing the design

Everything that defines him is at the top of `model.mjs`, in three plainly named objects:

- `SHAPE`: the size of the head and how deep it sits, the number, size and reach of the arms, the height of the line between the two colours, and where the face sits.
- `FACES`: the eyes, lids, brows, mouth and cheeks of each face, feature by feature, in the coordinates of the 2D drawings of `design/build-poulpy.mjs`.
- `MOODS`: for each of his six moods, the side of the plush and the face he makes. `buildPoulpy3d({ mood, tone })` builds him in one, tinted with that mood's `mood` token.
- `LOOK`: the light, the shadow band, the gleam and the outline.

His poses are in `poses.mjs`: `POSES` gives each one its mood, its face, what he wears, what lies on his head and the effects around him, all in the drawing's coordinates, and `buildPoulpyPose({ pose })` builds it.

How he moves is in `motion.mjs`: `TURN` for his turn, `JUMP` for his jump, `CHEER` for the champion's, `WINK` for the cocky one's and `RIG` for where the arms bend.

His colours are the `color.poulpy` tokens of [`design/tokens.json`](https://github.com/tkint/coach-poulpy/blob/main/design/tokens.json), never written into the model.

Edit, then regenerate and look at the workshop's page (`npm run storybook` in `web/`):

```sh
node design/build-poulpy-3d.mjs
```

CI runs it with `--check` and fails when the committed glTF files no longer match `model.mjs` and `motion.mjs`. A new mood in 3D starts as a new entry in `FACES` and `MOODS`, a new pose as a new entry in `POSES`; a new move starts next to `TURN`, `JUMP`, `CHEER` and `WINK` in `motion.mjs`, with a page of its own in the workshop. Both follow the [brand rules](?path=/docs/brand-character--docs) like any drawing.
